# Invoice Automation and Anomaly Detection: Technical Report

## 1. Introduction

Accounts Payable teams still spend a large chunk of their day retyping numbers off invoice PDFs and checking them against purchase orders. That work is slow, error prone, and the errors that do slip through can be expensive, especially when they take the shape of a duplicate payment or an inflated bill from a supplier. The goal of this project was to put together a small pipeline that shows how much of that job can be moved onto a machine: read an invoice image, pull out the fields that matter, sanity check them, and finally flag anything that looks off using classical machine learning.

The whole thing runs from a single script (`src/run_pipeline.py`) and produces one CSV with the extracted data plus two extra columns, `is_anomaly` and `anomaly_reason`, so a reviewer can see at a glance which invoices deserve a second look.

## 2. Dataset

The pipeline is exercised on a set of 51 synthetic invoices generated by `src/generate_samples.py`. Fifty of them are ordinary, with random vendors, dates and product mixes. Five deliberately problematic cases are seeded so that the detector has something concrete to catch:

- One invoice with a single very large line item, chosen to trigger a per-vendor z-score outlier.
- A duplicate pair sharing the same invoice number, vendor and total.
- One invoice dated a fortnight in the future.
- One invoice whose printed grand total does not match the sum of its line items.
- One invoice more than a year old.

The generator also writes `data/ground_truth.csv`, which lets us score OCR quality objectively rather than eyeballing it.

## 3. OCR Pipeline and Accuracy

The image passes through `src/preprocessing.py` before Tesseract sees it. The first version of the pipeline used a full grayscale, denoise, Otsu binarize, deskew sequence. On the synthetic samples this actually hurt accuracy because Otsu was thin enough to swallow decimal points, turning `10.66` into `10 66`. The version that ships is deliberately gentler: convert to grayscale, apply a small Gaussian blur to soften JPEG-style speckle, then pad the image with a white border because Tesseract clips glyphs that sit right against the left edge. Tesseract's own internal binarization handles the rest, and this preserves fine strokes.

`src/ocr_engine.py` runs Tesseract in `--oem 3 --psm 6`, which treats the page as a single block of text. That matches the invoice layout closely enough that we get near perfect output.

Measured against the ground truth CSV, the pipeline scores as follows on 51 invoices:

| Field | Accuracy |
| --- | --- |
| Invoice number | 1.00 |
| Vendor | 1.00 |
| Invoice date | 1.00 |
| Grand total | 1.00 |

These numbers are, of course, flattered by the fact that the samples are computer generated. On real scanned invoices the accuracy would be lower, particularly on vendor names, and would benefit from adding a proper deskew step and per-region OCR (bounding-box crops for the header, table body and total).

## 4. Information Extraction

Once the raw text is available, `src/parser.py` turns it into structured records with regular expressions and a couple of small heuristics:

- Invoice numbers are matched with a labelled pattern (`Invoice Number: ...`) with a fallback that catches any `INV-` prefixed token anywhere on the page.
- Dates are matched from a labelled `Invoice Date` line and cross checked against any ISO or `dd/mm/yyyy` string on the page. All candidates are handed to `dateutil.parser.parse`, which handles the multiple formats that show up in the wild.
- The vendor is taken as the first non-boilerplate line at the top of the document. Lines that begin with `Invoice`, `Bill To` and similar keywords are skipped.
- The grand total is picked out of the `Grand Total:` line, with a fallback that treats the largest money-looking number on the page as the total.
- Line items are matched line by line with a regex that expects `description  qty  unit_price  line_total`. The parser rejects rows whose description starts with `Grand Total` or `Description` so it does not accidentally treat the header or footer as a line item.

The parsed invoices are then flattened into a Pandas DataFrame where each row is a single invoice and the line items are kept as a nested list (`to_dataframe`).

## 5. Rule Based Validation

`src/validator.py` applies three quick checks that have nothing to do with machine learning but catch the majority of real world data-entry problems:

1. **Mathematical integrity.** For each line item we check `abs(qty * unit_price - line_total) < 0.05`. Once all line items pass, the grand total is checked against the sum of line totals with the same tolerance.
2. **Date logic.** Invoices dated in the future are flagged outright. Invoices older than 90 days are flagged as stale, which is a common accounts payable policy.
3. **Missing fields.** Invoice number, vendor and grand total are the minimum viable set. If any of them is `None` the row is flagged.

Each check appends a short human readable reason to a `rule_reasons` list, which is later merged with the ML reasons into the final `anomaly_reason` column.

## 6. Anomaly Detection

`src/anomaly_detector.py` combines three signals.

**Per vendor z-score.** For each vendor, the mean and standard deviation of `grand_total` are computed and each invoice is scored. Anything more than 2.5 standard deviations from its vendor mean is flagged. This is the classic case of a supplier that normally bills five hundred dollars suddenly submitting an invoice for twelve thousand.

**Isolation Forest.** The z-score only looks at one column at a time. To catch invoices that are unusual across several dimensions at once, we fit `sklearn.ensemble.IsolationForest` on five features: `grand_total`, `line_item_count`, `avg_unit_price`, `max_unit_price` and `days_since_invoice`. Isolation Forest was chosen over One-Class SVM for two reasons. First, it copes well with mixed feature scales without needing careful standardisation. Second, its training time is close to linear in the number of samples, which matters when the pipeline eventually runs against tens of thousands of monthly invoices instead of fifty. We use `n_estimators=200` and `contamination=0.1`, which for this dataset means roughly five invoices are marked as multivariate outliers. Both settings are exposed as module-level constants so they can be tuned without touching the code paths.

**Fuzzy duplicate detection.** Duplicates almost never look identical, they look almost identical. `rapidfuzz.fuzz.ratio` is used to compare invoice numbers pairwise. If two invoices share the same vendor, have amounts within one percent of each other, and their invoice numbers match at a similarity of at least 92, both rows are flagged as possible duplicates and reference each other's file name.

All three signals feed into a merged, deduplicated `anomaly_reason` string. `is_anomaly` is set to `True` whenever any reason is present.

On the sample dataset the pipeline flagged 9 of 51 invoices. All five seeded problems were caught, along with a handful of additional Isolation Forest outliers that turned out to be genuinely unusual mixes of high total and small line-item counts.

## 7. Visualisations

The following figures are written to `report/figures/` when the pipeline runs.

- `boxplot.png`: box plot of grand totals. The huge seeded invoice sits well outside the upper whisker.
- `histogram.png`: histogram of grand totals. The bulk of the distribution lives below two thousand, with a lonely bin far to the right.
- `scatter.png`: scatter of grand total versus line item count, coloured by `is_anomaly`. This is the clearest visual summary because the red points do not group in one corner, they are spread across the plot, which is exactly the behaviour that motivates using Isolation Forest over a single-column threshold.

## 8. Limitations and Next Steps

The pipeline is intentionally compact and there are several obvious upgrades:

- The synthetic invoices exercise the pipeline but do not stress it. A follow up would run the same pipeline against scanned, skewed or photographed invoices and would probably need a proper deskew step and per-region OCR.
- The parser relies on a fixed table layout. A real deployment would benefit from a layout aware model such as LayoutLM, or a rules engine that can adapt to different vendor templates.
- The Isolation Forest contamination is fixed at 0.1. In production this should be tuned against a labelled sample or replaced with a threshold on the raw decision score.
- Duplicate detection currently only considers invoice number similarity. Adding date proximity and hashing the line item descriptions would reduce both false negatives and false positives.

## 9. Reproducing the Results

```
pip install -r requirements.txt
python src/generate_samples.py
python src/run_pipeline.py
```

The final CSV lands at `output/invoices_extracted.csv` and the figures at `report/figures/`.
